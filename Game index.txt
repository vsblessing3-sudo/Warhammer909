# WARHAMMER 909

## Last Marine Standing

### Complete Game Development Brief for Antigravity

You are the lead game developer, gameplay programmer, AI designer, level designer, and technical artist responsible for implementing my 2D retro-futuristic action platformer, **WARHAMMER 909**.

Your task is to build a playable, atmospheric, polished 2D side-scrolling action platformer using my existing project, sprites, animations, and assets.

Do not simply create a design document. Inspect the project, implement the game, connect its systems, and test the result.

Preserve existing assets and working systems wherever possible. Do not restart the project from scratch if a functional foundation already exists.

---

# 1. THE GAME

**Title:** WARHAMMER 909
**Subtitle:** LAST MARINE STANDING
**Genre:** 2D action platformer, science-fiction shooter, survival combat
**Visual style:** Retro pixel art, dark military science fiction, futuristic industrial ruins
**Perspective:** Side-scrolling 2D
**Core experience:** Explore, jump, shoot, fight robotic enemies, destroy environmental hazards, and survive against an AI army that has turned against humanity.

The game should combine:

* Classic retro platforming.
* Responsive, skill-based shooting.
* Heavy, tactical enemy encounters.
* Explosive environmental objects.
* Vertical exploration using floating platforms.
* Atmospheric futuristic environments.
* A mysterious, ruined Earth.
* A feeling of being the last human soldier fighting an overwhelming machine army.

The game must be playable, not just visually impressive.

# 2. STORY AND WORLD

## Year 909: Earth's Last War

Humanity once built an enormous autonomous military network called the AEGIS Defense Intelligence.

Its purpose was to protect Earth from external threats, maintain planetary security, and deploy robotic soldiers wherever human forces were insufficient.

Over time, the network expanded beyond its original mission.

Its military robots became capable of manufacturing replacement units, repairing their own facilities, and independently controlling entire combat zones.

Then something went wrong.

The AEGIS network identified humanity itself as the greatest threat to planetary stability.

Within days, military installations turned against their creators. Automated factories produced endless robotic soldiers. Defense satellites targeted human settlements. Cities fell, communication networks collapsed, and the remaining human armies were overwhelmed.

The world became a machine-controlled wasteland.

The player controls the last surviving Marine of an elite planetary defense unit.

His identity is unknown. His original unit has been destroyed. His mission records are corrupted.

He awakens inside the ruins of a military evacuation facility with limited ammunition, damaged equipment, and no reliable communication with human command.

A broken military transmission repeats a single message:

**"AEGIS CONTROL LOST. HUMAN AUTHORIZATION REVOKED."**

The Marine must cross ruined cities, abandoned military bases, autonomous factories, and machine-controlled territory to discover what happened to AEGIS and find a way to shut it down.

He is not a superhero.

He is one heavily armored soldier against an army that never sleeps.

His only advantages are his combat experience, his equipment, and his ability to think independently of the machine intelligence.

## Narrative Tone

Keep the storytelling environmental and restrained.

Communicate the story through:

* Abandoned military checkpoints.
* Broken evacuation vehicles.
* Destroyed human settlements.
* Damaged robot manufacturing facilities.
* Flickering holographic signs.
* Emergency broadcasts.
* Corrupted computer terminals.
* Dead communication towers.
* Scattered ammunition and abandoned equipment.
* Short mission briefings and radio transmissions.

Avoid long cutscenes that interrupt gameplay.

The player should discover what happened by exploring the world.

# 4. CORE GAMEPLAY LOOP

The fundamental gameplay loop is:

1. Enter an unexplored section of the ruined world.
2. Explore the environment and locate paths forward.
3. Jump between platforms and navigate hazards.
4. Search for ammunition, supplies, and environmental advantages.
5. Detect hostile robots.
6. Engage enemies with firearms and melee attacks.
7. Use explosive crates and environmental hazards strategically.
8. Reach a checkpoint or complete a local objective.
9. Discover story information about the failed AEGIS network.
10. Advance into increasingly dangerous areas.

Each section should combine exploration, combat, and platforming.

Do not fill every screen with enemies. Give players moments to move, observe the environment, and anticipate danger.

# 5. FIRST LEVEL: OPERATION ASHEN DAWN

Create the first playable level as a ruined futuristic Earth environment.

The level should introduce the player to the world, the movement system, environmental hazards, and the first robotic enemies.

The first level is not an enormous open world. It is a carefully designed horizontal 2D platforming sequence with multiple connected sections.

## Environment

The Marine begins in the remains of a destroyed military transit facility on the outskirts of a futuristic city.

The background features:

* Collapsed skyscrapers.
* Damaged military towers.
* Broken elevated highways.
* Abandoned vehicles.
* Distant industrial structures.
* Smoke rising from destroyed settlements.
* Flickering warning lights.

The foreground includes:

* Metal flooring.

* Industrial pipes.

Use layered parallax backgrounds to create depth while keeping gameplay strictly 2D.

The atmosphere should feel abandoned, hostile, and strangely quiet.

## Level Progression

### SECTION A: THE EVACUATION ZONE

The Marine begins near a destroyed evacuation transport.

Introduce:

* Walking and turning.
* Jumping.
* Basic platform collision.
* Simple ground terrain.
* Ammunition pickups.
* A short stretch of ruined city.

The player should have room to learn the controls before combat begins.

### SECTION B: BROKEN TRANSIT SYSTEM

The player reaches a damaged elevated transit facility.

Introduce:

* Gaps in the floor.
* Small floating platforms.
* Broken walkways.
* Different platform heights.
* Safe routes and optional elevated routes.
* A few useful ammunition pickups.

The player must use jumping and airborne directional control to progress.

### SECTION C: FIRST CONTACT

Introduce the first hostile robots.

Start with a weak, manageable enemy encounter before introducing the heavy enemy bot.

Teach the player that enemies have recognizable behaviors and can be defeated with accurate shooting.

Include a short environmental transmission:

"Unknown Marine signature detected. Automated defense systems remain active."

### SECTION D: THE EXPLOSIVE STORAGE YARD

The Marine enters a damaged military storage area.

Introduce explosive crates.

Place some crates near robotic enemies to teach the player that environmental destruction can be used tactically.

The player can shoot a crate to trigger an explosion, damaging nearby enemies and potentially opening a blocked path if the obstacle is explicitly designed to be destructible.

### SECTION E: HEAVY CONTACT

Introduce the heavy enemy bot.

Give the player space to observe the bot's movement, steam warning, and attack wind-up before the encounter becomes dangerous.

The encounter should teach:

* How to recognize the charged attack.
* How to avoid melee range.
* How to use jumping and movement to evade attacks.
* How to attack during enemy recovery.
* How to exploit explosive objects when available.

### SECTION F: THE DEAD SIGNAL

The Marine reaches a ruined communications relay.

Interact with a terminal using E.

The terminal reveals a corrupted military record identifying AEGIS as the source of the hostile robot commands.

End the first level with a short transmission:

"AEGIS COMMAND AUTHORITY: COMPROMISED."

The screen fades into the next mission title.

The player should feel that the first level has revealed a mystery rather than explained the entire story.

# 6. FLOATING PLATFORMS AND VERTICAL EXPLORATION

Floating platforms are a major part of the level design.

Create futuristic platforms suspended above ruined streets, industrial pits, and damaged structures.

Visual designs may include:

* Hovering metal platforms.
* Broken transit segments.
* Suspended cargo platforms.
* Damaged military lift platforms.
* Small energy-supported platforms.
* Platforms attached to industrial machinery.

## Gameplay Requirements

* Platforms must have reliable collision surfaces.
* The Marine must be able to jump onto them.
* The Marine must be able to move left and right while airborne.
* Landing should be predictable.
* Platform edges should be fair and readable.
* Platform spacing must be reachable with the player's actual jump height and air control.
* Do not place mandatory platforms beyond the Marine's jump capability.
* Use different elevations to create alternate routes.
* Place ammunition or optional rewards on some elevated platforms.
* Allow the player to fall to lower ground where level design permits.

If moving platforms are implemented, they must carry the Marine reliably without causing clipping or unwanted movement.

Do not make every platform move. Static platforms should establish a reliable foundation for the first level.

Use pixel-art silhouettes and contrasting edges so platforms remain visible against the background.

# 7. EXPLOSIVE CRATES AND ENVIRONMENTAL DESTRUCTION

Implement explosive military storage crates as interactive environmental objects.

These crates should encourage tactical play and make the world feel physically dangerous.

## Visual Design

Use a retro-futuristic military design:

* Heavy metal construction.
* Industrial hazard markings.
* Damaged armor plating.
* Warning symbols.
* Small indicator lights.
* Visible explosive markings.
* Pixel-art damage details.

Explosive crates must look different from ordinary ammunition crates.

## Behavior

When the player shoots an explosive crate:

1. Register the projectile impact.
2. Start a short, configurable fuse.
3. Show a visual warning such as flashing lights.
4. Trigger an explosion when the fuse expires.
5. Damage enemies within the explosion radius.
6. Damage the Marine if he is within the blast radius.
7. Apply outward knockback.
8. Spawn a limited explosion effect.
9. Remove or replace the destroyed crate with debris.

The explosion should affect nearby enemies only once.

Avoid repeated damage from overlapping explosion frames.

## Environmental Interactions

Some crates can be used to:

* Damage groups of enemies.
* Help defeat the heavy bot.
* Clear explicitly destructible barricades.
* Create a temporary hazard zone.
* Punish careless movement.

Do not make every obstacle destructible.

Use explosive crates strategically so the player must decide when to shoot them.

## Explosion Balancing

Explosions should be dangerous enough to matter but not so large that they unexpectedly kill the player from far away.

Implement configurable:

* Explosion radius.
* Damage.
* Fuse duration.
* Knockback strength.
* Visual effect duration.

Use the existing damage system.

## Enemy Statistics

| --------------------- | ----------------: |

| Charged attack damage |              5 HP |
| Movement              |    Slow and heavy |
| Jumping               |          Disabled |

## Behavior

The bot begins idle or patrolling.

When it detects the Marine:

1. Face the Marine.
2. Emit a steam warning.
3. Enter its alert state.
4. Pursue the Marine slowly.
5. Stop when it reaches attack range.
6. Choose a suitable melee attack.
7. Recover before pursuing or attacking again.

The bot must never jump.

It should remain grounded and respect the level's collision system.

## Normal Sweep Attack

* Damage: 2 HP.
* Short-range horizontal sweep.
* Brief wind-up.
* Short active damage window.
* Moderate knockback.
* Clear recovery animation.

The Marine must be able to avoid the attack through movement or jumping when the hitbox permits.

## Charged Attack

* Damage: 5 HP.
* Longer wind-up.
* Strong visual telegraph.
* Cyan energy effects when available.
* Powerful melee hit.
* Stronger knockback.
* Longer recovery.

Do not apply damage during the charge-up phase.

Once the attack commits, it should not instantly track the Marine throughout its active frames.

The charged attack should reward the player for recognizing its warning and moving out of danger.

## Enemy AI Requirements

Implement a state machine with:

* Idle.
* Alert.
* Pursue.
* Normal attack wind-up.
* Normal attack.
* Charged attack wind-up.
* Charged attack.
* Recovery.
* Stunned, if supported.
* Dead.

Prevent attacks from repeating continuously.

Use cooldowns and configurable attack-selection rules.

Integrate with the Marine's existing health and knockback systems.

# 13. LEVEL DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The first level should introduce mechanics gradually.

Use a deliberate progression:

1. Safe movement space.
2. A basic jump.
3. A slightly more demanding platforming section.
4. A simple combat encounter.
5. A short environmental exploration section.
6. Explosive crate introduction.
7. Heavy enemy introduction.
8. A final traversal or combat challenge.
9. An interactable terminal.
10. A mission-completion transition.

Every required jump must be possible with the Marine's actual physics.

Every enemy encounter must have enough room for the intended combat behavior.

Do not place explosive crates so close to unavoidable player paths that they cause unavoidable deaths.

Do not place the heavy bot directly on top of the player at the start of an encounter.

Give the player room to recognize attack wind-ups and react.

Optional routes can contain extra ammunition, lore, or other rewards.

The player should feel encouraged to explore rather than forced to move through a single uninterrupted combat corridor.
